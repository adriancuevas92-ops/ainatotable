# Glossary

Plain-language definitions for every term used across this site. Every article links back here the first time it uses one of these words.

**Canopy layer** — The tallest layer of a food forest, made up of full-size fruit and nut trees. Forms the top of the system and shapes light conditions for every layer below it.

**Chop-and-drop** — Cutting back a fast-growing plant (often a nitrogen fixer or groundcover) and leaving the cuttings on the ground where they fall, rather than removing them. The cuttings break down and feed the soil in place.

**Establishment / establishment period** — The stretch of time (typically one to three years) between planting and the point where a food forest can largely sustain itself — deep enough roots, closed enough canopy, healthy enough soil — without ongoing intensive care.

**Food forest** — A planted, multi-layer ecosystem grown for food, designed to organize itself the way a natural forest does, with plants placed to support each other rather than grown in isolated rows.

**Forest garden** — Used interchangeably with "food forest" in most sources; both terms describe the same layered, perennial-based growing system.

**Groundcover layer** — Low, spreading plants that cover bare soil, hold in moisture, and suppress weeds by taking up space weeds would otherwise fill.

**Guild** — A small group of plants placed together on purpose because they help each other — commonly a food-producing plant paired with a nitrogen fixer, a nutrient accumulator, and/or something that draws pollinators or deters pests.

**Herbaceous layer** — Non-woody perennial plants — vegetables and herbs — filling in at and slightly above ground level.

**Nitrogen fixer** — A plant (often a legume tree or shrub, such as koa) that pulls nitrogen from the air and makes it available in the soil, feeding other plants growing near it.

**Nutrient accumulator** — A deep-rooted plant that pulls minerals up from deeper in the soil than most other plants can reach, making those nutrients available near the surface.

**Permaculture** — A design approach that models growing systems on how natural ecosystems already sustain themselves. The framework behind food forest design, though you don't need the term to use the ideas.

**Permaculture zones** — A way of laying out a site by how often each part is visited: zone 0 is the house, zone 1 is visited daily, and zones 2 through 5 are visited progressively less often. Used to decide where high-maintenance vs. low-maintenance plantings belong.

**Polyculture** — Growing many different species together in the same space, as opposed to a monoculture (one crop, grown alone). A food forest is a deliberately designed polyculture.

**Root layer** — Crops grown underground — kalo (taro) is the primary example used throughout this site — using soil space that nothing else in the guild is competing for.

**Shrub layer** — Berry bushes and other shrub-scale food plants, filling the space between the tree layers and the ground layers.

**Succession planting** — Replacing an individual plant, once it dies or ages out of productivity, with something new — ideally filling the same role in its guild — so the system keeps functioning rather than developing a permanent gap.

**Understory layer** — Smaller or dwarf trees that fill the light gaps the canopy layer leaves open, tolerating partial shade better than full-size canopy trees would.

**Vine layer** — Climbing plants that use the vertical space on tree trunks or structures, rather than needing ground space or a trellis of their own.

**Wet-tropical** — The climate classification this site's current content is scoped to (Hawaiʻi and similar regions) — high rainfall, minimal frost risk, year-round growing season. Other climates will get their own content once there's real planting experience behind it.
