# Further Reading

Two kinds of resources: organizations already doing work like this, worth knowing about directly, and the published research behind specific claims made elsewhere on this site. Neither list is meant to be exhaustive — just real and worth your time.

## Organizations doing similar work

- [Permaculture Apprentice — How to Create a Food Forest](https://permacultureapprentice.com/creating-a-food-forest-step-by-step-guide/) — a solid, detailed step-by-step guide, useful to compare against the planning content on this site.
- [Agroforestry.org](https://agroforestry.org/) — a nonprofit that publishes *The Overstory*, a free long-running agroforestry journal. Good for going deeper than any single site can.
- [Portland Food Forest Initiative](https://pdxfoodforest.org/) — a nonprofit doing homeowner lawn-to-food-forest conversion, one of the closest analogues to this venture's homeowner-facing mission.
- [Project Food Forest](https://projectfoodforest.org/) — another nonprofit model, public food forests plus classes.
- [Denver Urban Gardens — Food Forests](https://dug.org/gardens-food-forests/food-forests/) — a community-orchard model, useful for seeing the "public good" framing done well.
- [OpenTable — Farm-to-Table Sourcing Guide](https://www.opentable.com/restaurant-solutions/resources/restaurant-guide-to-sustainable-sourcing/) — written for restaurant operators specifically.
- [WebstaurantStore — Local Food Sourcing Guide](https://www.webstaurantstore.com/article/1105/local-food-sourcing.html) — same audience, a more operational angle.

## Published research

Organized to mirror the rest of this site, so you can go straight to the research behind whatever claim you want to check. Some of these are peer-reviewed journal articles; a few are theses or graduate research projects, noted as such — real and useful, but held to a different standard than peer review.

**On food forest design**

- Allen & Mason (2021), [Urban food forests in the American Southwest](https://acsess.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/uar2.20018)
- Albrecht & Wiek (2021), [Food forests: Their services and sustainability](https://www.foodsystemsjournal.org/index.php/fsj/article/download/985/959)
- Nair (1985), [Classification of agroforestry systems](https://openalex.org/W2080189260)
- Lovell, Dupraz, Gold et al. (2017), [Temperate agroforestry research: multifunctional woody polycultures and long-term field trial design](https://openalex.org/W2601752462)
- Riolo (2018), [The social and environmental value of the Picasso Food Forest, Parma, Italy](https://openalex.org/W2895072883)
- Nero, Kwapong et al. (2018), [Tree species diversity and socioeconomic perspectives of urban food trees, Accra, Ghana](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/10/10/3417/pdf?version=1537876189)

**On direct farm-to-restaurant supply chains**

- Paciarotti & Torregiani (2018), [Short food supply chain between micro/small farms and restaurants](https://openalex.org/W2887433927)
- Paciarotti, Mazzuto, Torregiani et al. (2022), [Locally produced food for restaurants: a supply chain network design](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/IJRDM-10-2021-0477/full/pdf)
- Jump (2019, graduate thesis), [Understanding local produce sourcing by restaurateurs in Houston, Texas](https://core.ac.uk/download/212904006.pdf)
- Török, Agárdi, Maró et al. (2022), [Business opportunities in short food supply chains: three Hungarian para-gastro restaurants](https://studies.hu/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/2253_Torok.pdf)
- Buck, Getz & Guthman (1997), [From Farm to Table: The Organic Vegetable Commodity Chain of Northern California](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/1467-9523.00033) — dense and academic, but the earliest well-cited look at direct/organic commodity chains in this literature.

**On soil, water, and establishment**

- Fahad, Chavan, Chichaghare et al. (2022), [Agroforestry systems for soil health improvement and maintenance](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/14/22/14877/pdf?version=1669103852)
- Muthuri, Barrios, Öborn et al. (2015), [Local knowledge on soil health indicators in Kenyan agroforestry](https://core.ac.uk/download/700041332.pdf) — smallholder, tropical-farm scale, closer to this venture's own scale than most Western research.
- Montagnini & Nair (2004), [Carbon sequestration in agroforestry systems](https://openalex.org/W2139191427)

**On native forest restoration**

- Friday, Cordell & Giardina (2015), [Future directions for forest restoration in Hawaiʻi](https://openalex.org/W1107265831) — the one Hawaiʻi-specific paper in this whole list, and the closest thing to direct academic grounding for the restoration work behind the [Nā Hoa Ululāʻau case study](case-study-na-hoa-ululaau.md).

**On indigenous stewardship and traditional ecological knowledge**

- Berkes, Colding & Folke (2000), [Rediscovery of Traditional Ecological Knowledge as Adaptive Management](https://openalex.org/W2161586297) — the foundational paper in this field, cited thousands of times since.
- Robbins & Berkes (2000), [Sacred Ecology: Traditional Ecological Knowledge and Resource Management](https://openalex.org/W1983786851)
- Chan (2022, graduate research), [Solutions to California's Wildfires: Indigenous Stewardship and Traditional Ecological Knowledge](https://core.ac.uk/download/543812693.pdf)
- Wilson & Noyes (2025), [Indigenous Stewardship of Ancestral Lands Activates Land and Culture](https://core.ac.uk/download/637018951.pdf) — on Bears Ears National Monument's co-stewardship model.
- Martinez (2023, graduate research), [We Shape the Land: Environmental Decision Making and Climate Justice](https://core.ac.uk/download/591735125.pdf) — California Native land stewardship tied directly to food and culture.

None of the traditional-ecological-knowledge research above is Hawaiʻi- or Kanaka Maoli-specific — worth saying plainly rather than letting the citations imply otherwise. This site's own laulima and mālama ʻāina content (see [About](about-contact.md)) comes from family and cultural knowledge, not from these papers. They're included here as general grounding for why traditional ecological knowledge matters as a field, not as a substitute for Hawaiian sourcing.

**On labor and cooperative structures**

- Arana Landín (2019), [A study of the statutory background for worker cooperatives in the US](https://baidc.revistas.deusto.es/article/download/1424/1942)
- Martin, Martin & Martin (2014), [Social Reform and Worker Cooperatives: Countering Economic Inequality](https://openalex.org/W793440355)
